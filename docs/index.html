I used a personal-size Big Cola bottle, about a quarter full of vinegar, and half a small box of baking soda. Here is one explicit calculation.

Vinegar

Loaded mass before release≈ 185 g

About 100 g vinegar + 25 g baking soda + 60 g rocket. Package sizes and dry mass were not verified. A 400–500 mL bottle and 40–80 g dry rocket give about 165–230 g loaded, with baking soda held at 25 g.

The next test needs
better measurements.

“I would start from the lighter version, weigh each part and change one thing at a time.”

Vinegar and baking soda produce carbon dioxide. As gas builds up, pressure rises. Escaping liquid and gas push the rocket in the opposite direction.

Added mass, leaks, tilt and friction with the support could all affect the flight. These tests do not isolate a single cause.

What about the 5.1 m calculation?

That is an ideal example, separate from the 5 m goal. An assumed upward speed of 10 m/s after thrust ends gives an extra rise of 10² ÷ (2 × 9.81) ≈ 5.1 m without air resistance. That speed was not measured.
