# Water rocket project

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Delegated by the user's instruction to complete the project without questions. Static HTML, CSS and JavaScript, published through GitHub Pages.

## Users

Santiago and the audience reviewing his STEAM school project. A teacher reviewing the submission is an inferred primary visitor.

## Product Purpose

Present the real rocket, its construction, scientific explanation, documentary, test results and conclusion directly on the website.

## Capabilities and Constraints

The site must show real photographs and the corrected final-launch recording. Reported heights are approximately 2 m, 0.5 m and 1 m; the goal is at least 5 m. Material quantities and mass use explicitly stated assumptions. The website, documentary narration and graphics, captions and standalone report must all be Spanish, following Santiago's schoolwork writing profile. English chat or code conventions do not change the language of project content. The website needs fuller explanatory text, no document-download promotion and no teacher grading-criteria block; the report is a separate submission. The report requires a proper cover and all 15 original photographs, with no embedded video. No login or backend is needed.

## Brand Commitments

Continue the documentary's lively blue, yellow and green identity, with large readable headings and real project material. The separate school report uses a plain black Arial layout.

## Evidence on Hand

`revision-v6/public/design/` and `revision-v6/public/media/` contain project photography and prepared clips. `revision-v6/narration/revision-plan.json` records the final release, quantities and results. `revision-v7/evidence/` contains all 15 report photographs, with original-source hashes in its manifest. `Rocket-Complete-Report-v7.docx` and `.pdf` contain the complete Spanish submission.

## Product Principles

- Let the actual experiment lead.
- Keep estimates visibly distinct from measurements.
- Explain the project on the page and give the documentary direct, working access.
- Make the page readable on phones and classroom computers.
